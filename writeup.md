Advanced Lane Finding Project The goals / steps of this project are the following: 
    Compute the camera calibration matrix and distortion coefﬁcients given a set of chessboard images. 
    Apply a distortion correction to raw images. Use color transforms, gradients, etc., to create a thresholded binary image. 
    Apply a perspective transform to rectify binary image ("birds-eye view"). 
    Detect lane pixels and ﬁt to ﬁnd the lane boundary. 
    Determine the curvature of the lane and vehicle position with respect to center. 
    Warp the detected lane boundaries back onto the original image. 
    Output visual display of the lane boundaries and numerical estimation of lane curvature and vehicle position.

Camera Calibration
1)Briefly state how you computed the camera matrix and distortion coefficients. Provide an example of a distortion corrected calibration image. Provide an example of a distortion-corrected image.

In Cell 2 of Advanced_lane_finding.ipynb, I used the cv2.findChessboardCorners function to find the chessboard corners for the 9x6 chessboard images in the camera_cal folder.  For some of the calibration images, corners could not be found so those images were ignored.  A list of all the found chessboard corners was used to create distortion coefficients with the cv2.calibrateCamera function and compute the distortion corrected images using the cv2.undistort function in Cells 3,4 & 5.  Distortion corrected images are shown in the output of theses cells and in the undistorted_test_images folder.

2)Describe how (and identify where in your code) you used color transforms, gradients or other methods to create a thresholded binary image. Provide an example of a binary image result.

I tried a number of different methods using the Sobel operator for creating thresholded binary images: x-oriented Sobel gradient (Cell 7), Sobel magnitude (Cell 8), Sobel gradient direction (Cell 9), and combined magnitue and direction (Cells 10 & 11).  I also experimented with combinations of the HSL and RGB components (Cells 11 - 14).  In Cells 15 & 16, I tried combinations of saturation and Sobel x gradients.  After warping to a bird's eye view (Cell 22), I didn't like the results that these combination methods created, particularly with light colored pavements, shadows, and yellow lines.  Yellow lines seem to disappear when the distances are far and on light colored pavement.  Shadows cause the saturation to be high and appear in the binary images as noise.  Light colored pavements cause the Sobel results to be poor.  Random skid marks and stains on the pavement are detected by the Sobel operator and added noise to the binary image.  In Cells 23 & 24, I experiment with multiplying the Red (R) and Green (G) components. R*G and (R*G)^2 seems to bring the yellow and white lines to the foreground.  In Cell 25 (function draw_window_lane_pixels) and the more refined function get_lane_pixels in Cell 26, I  dynamically changed the threshold for identifying lane line pixels using each window's average and 85% percentile to compensate for changes in the background pavement color.  Light colored pavement windows would get high thresholds for the binary image.  Pixels outside the right and left line windows would be discarded.  The "lane pixels" images in Cell 25's output show the thresholded binary images with left lane pixels in red and right lane pixels in blue.

3)Describe how (and identify where in your code) you performed a perspective transform and provide an example of a transformed image.

The perspective transformation function are determined in Cells 20 & 21.  I used the previous projects functions in Cells 17 & 19 to determine best fit lane lines for each image, then used those lines to create the corners of the trapezoid (src and dst) needed to obtain the perspective transform matrix using cv2.getPerspectiveTransform.  Function recenter_src_dst in Cell 20 was used to get src and dst from the right and left lane lines generated by previous project's extract_lane_lines function in Cell 21.  Averaged src and dst arrays across the test images was used to create the prespective transformation matrix, M_ave.  In Cell 22, I outputted the bird's eye images in the unwarped_test_images folder.  Please note I had to increase the width the bird's eye images from 1280 to 3000 cause the lane lines were curving off the image.

4)Describe how (and identify where in your code) you identified lane-line pixels and fit their positions with a polynomial?

The first pass for finding lane windows is shown in Cell 25.  I used the convolution technique for determining the window centroids for the right and left lane lines using the unthresholded images as input.  Thresholds were adjusted and applied to each window to determine the lane-line pixels.  In Cell 26, I refined this methodology by using a band around the window centers with the function create_window_mask_using_curve rather than using discrete rectangular windows.  In the function get_lane_pixels, the thresholds are adjusted dynamically for each window and spline interpolated between windows centers.  The functions pixel_fit and curve_fit was used to fit the laneline pixels to parabolas.  To avoid poorly formed polynomials, these functions adjust the fits if there isn't enough points or if the vertex of the parabola is less than the max y extent of the image.  Cell 25's output has example images of the lane-lines' fitted polynomials, labeled as 'lane overlay' and 'best fit lane lines'.

5)Describe how (and identify where in your code) you calculated the radius of curvature of the lane and the position of the vehicle with respect to center.

In order to determine road curvature radius and vehicle position, the first thing I had to do is determine the meters per pixel conversion in the x and y directions.  In Cell 25, I looked at the fft's (discrete fouier transforms) of dashed lines and saw prominent non-noise peaks of 6, 5, 6, 5, 6, 5, & 5.  Therefore the average peak frequency is 5.43 for broken laneline patterns, and there are 720 pixels in the y-directions so the periodicity is 720/5.43=132.6 pixels. According to the California DOT standards for road surface markings on Page 6-26: http://www.dot.ca.gov/trafficops/camutcd/docs/TMChapter6.pdf the periodicity is 14.64m for these same broken laneline patterns. Therefore the meters per pixel in y dimension is 14.64/132.6. The average lane width is 909 pixels with lanes being 3.7m wide makes the meters per pixel in x dimension: 3.7/909.  Resulting curvature radii were pretty close to 1000m in the test images.

The function measure_curvature in Cell 26 gives the radius of curvature as determined at the bottom of the image (nearest to the car) using the parabolas for the right and left lane-lines.  Function add_radius_center_text in Cell 27 also determines how the vehicle is centered in the lane by calculating the x positions of the right and left lane-line polynomials at the bottom of the image and determining its averaged position relative to the image's x centered position.
